# Skateboard Ramp Manufacturing Cost

**Course:** Managerial Accounting — SDSU Fowler College of Business  
**Date:** December 2025

---

## Overview

End-to-end managerial accounting model for a fictional San Diego-based skateboard ramp manufacturer. The project covers product costing, overhead allocation, pricing strategy, shipping logistics, and CVP analysis to evaluate the financial viability of the business.

---

## Key Components

**Bill of Materials (BOM)**  
Raw material costs sourced from Home Depot and Lowe's for four ramp types: launch ramps, bank ramps, quarterpipes, and funboxes, plus two premium accessories (Skatelite and coping upgrades).

**Activity-Based Costing (ABC)**  
Overhead allocated across two cost pools — facility overhead (driven by direct labor hours) and indirect labor (driven by number of shipments). Ensures fair and accurate cost distribution per product.

**Pricing Strategy**  
Cost-plus pricing with a 40% markup as the baseline. Supported by downside/upside scenario pricing and a target contribution margin of 30%, benchmarked against adjacent industries.

**Shipping Zone Model**  
Four national zones from a San Diego origin (West, South, Midwest, Northeast). Weighted average shipping cost of ~$91/unit embedded in variable costs using a 40/25/20/15 sales mix assumption.

**CVP Analysis**  
- Weighted average selling price: ~$1,853/unit  
- Weighted average variable cost: ~$1,324/unit  
- Contribution margin ratio: 28.57%  
- Break-even: ~425 units / $788K revenue  

**Product Mix Optimization**  
Skatelite upgrades (43.9%) and Funboxes (37.2%) identified as the primary contribution margin drivers, accounting for over 81% of total profitability.

**Scenario & Sensitivity Analysis**  
Stress-tested margins under material cost inflation, higher labor/shipping costs, and reduced demand in long-haul regions. Core product mix remains contribution-positive under all scenarios.

## Key Takeaways

- Variable costs, especially materials and shipping, are the biggest margin risk
- Operating leverage kicks in strongly once fixed costs (~$225K/year) are covered
- Product mix decisions matter more than volume alone — high-margin accessories can outperform core products in total profit contribution
